# Problem 33: Digit Cancelling Fractions

https://projecteuler.net/problem=33

**Answer:** 100 · **Solution:** `P33.java` · **Runtime:** < 0.01 s

## Problem

The fraction 49/98 is a curious fraction, as an inexperienced mathematician in attempting to simplify it may
incorrectly believe that 49/98 = 4/8, which is correct, is obtained by cancelling the 9s.

We shall consider fractions like, 30/50 = 3/5, to be trivial examples.

There are exactly four non-trivial examples of this type of fraction, less than one in value, and containing
two digits in the numerator and denominator.

If the product of these four fractions is given in its lowest common terms, find the value of the denominator.

## In Plain Terms

Some fractions can be "simplified" the wrong way — crossing out a digit that appears on top and bottom — and
still come out right by luck: 49/98, cross out the 9s, 4/8, and it really does equal 49/98. Check every
two-digit fraction less than 1 for this coincidence (skipping boring ones with zeros), multiply the four you
find, and reduce.

## In Theory

For a fraction i/j with i < j, both two-digit and no zeros, there are four ways a digit could be shared
(units/units, units/tens, tens/units, tens/tens). Test whether the fraction with the shared digit removed
equals the original. The four hits are

```
16/64 = 1/4    19/95 = 1/5    26/65 = 2/5    49/98 = 4/8
```

Their product is (1·1·2·4)/(4·5·5·8) = 8/800 = **1/100**.

## In Code

1. Double loop over i < j in 11..99, skipping multiples of 10.
2. For each of the four digit-sharing patterns, compare `i/j` with the reduced fraction as `double`s; on a
   match multiply `i` into `n_product` and `j` into `d_product`.
3. Reduce: factor both products with `Primes.getPrimeFactors`, subtract the numerator's exponents from the
   denominator's, and multiply out what's left of the denominator.

## Complexity

O(n²) over two-digit pairs — about 3,600 checks. Space O(1).

## Further Thoughts

Comparing `double`s with `==` happens to be safe here, but cross-multiplying (`i × b == j × a`) avoids
floating point entirely, and `gcd` reduces the final fraction in one step.
