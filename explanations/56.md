# Problem 56: Powerful Digit Sum

https://projecteuler.net/problem=56

**Answer:** 972 · **Solution:** `P56.java` · **Runtime:** ~0.09 s

## Problem

A googol (10¹⁰⁰) is a massive number: one followed by one-hundred zeros; 100¹⁰⁰ is almost unimaginably large:
one followed by two-hundred zeros. Despite their size, the sum of the digits in each number is only 1.

Considering natural numbers of the form, aᵇ, where a, b < 100, what is the maximum digital sum?

## In Plain Terms

Compute every a to the power b for a and b below 100 — almost ten thousand huge numbers — add up each one's
digits, and keep the biggest total. Java's big-integer type handles the size; the rest is just a double loop.

## In Theory

A d-digit number has digit sum at most 9d, and a "typical" one averages 4.5d. The largest values have about
b × log₁₀ a ≈ 198 digits, so we'd expect the best sums somewhere near 4.5 × 198 ≈ 890, with luck pushing it
higher. The winner is 99⁹⁵, whose digits sum to 972.

## In Code

1. For each `a` and `b` from 99 down to 1, compute `BigInteger.valueOf(a).pow(b)`.
2. Sum its digit characters and track the maximum.

## Complexity

O(n² × d²) for n² powers with d-digit multiplication — small at n = 99. Space O(d).

## Further Thoughts

Multiplying the previous power by `a` instead of calling `pow(b)` from scratch saves work, and the search could
start from large a and b, since small bases can't produce enough digits to compete.
