# Problem 18: Maximum Path Sum I

https://projecteuler.net/problem=18

**Answer:** 1074 · **Solution:** `P18.java` · **Runtime:** < 0.01 s

## Problem

By starting at the top of the triangle below and moving to adjacent numbers on the row below, the maximum
total from top to bottom is 23.

```
   3
  7 4
 2 4 6
8 5 9 3
```

That is, 3 + 7 + 4 + 9 = 23.

Find the maximum total from top to bottom of the triangle below:

```
                            75
                          95  64
                        17  47  82
                      18  35  87  10
                    20  04  82  47  65
                  19  01  23  75  03  34
                88  02  77  73  07  63  67
              99  65  04  28  06  16  70  92
            41  41  26  56  83  40  80  70  33
          41  48  72  33  47  32  37  16  94  29
        53  71  44  65  25  43  91  52  97  51  14
      70  11  33  28  77  73  17  78  39  68  17  57
    91  71  52  38  17  14  91  43  58  50  27  29  48
  63  66  04  68  89  53  67  30  73  16  69  87  40  31
04  62  98  27  23  09  70  98  73  93  38  53  60  04  23
```

NOTE: As there are only 16384 routes, it is possible to solve this problem by trying every route. However,
Problem 67 is the same challenge with a triangle containing one-hundred rows; it cannot be solved by brute
force, and requires a clever method!

## In Plain Terms

Instead of trying every route, work row by row and, for each number, write down the best total you could
possibly have when you arrive there. A number can only be reached from the one or two numbers directly above
it, so its best total is its own value plus the better of those. Once you've filled in the bottom row, the
biggest number written there is the answer.

## In Theory

This is **dynamic programming**. Let best(r, c) be the largest total of any path from the top to row r,
position c:

```
best(0, 0) = value(0, 0)
best(r, c) = value(r, c) + max(best(r−1, c−1), best(r−1, c))     (edges have only one parent)
answer     = max over c of best(last row, c)
```

Each cell's best depends only on the row above, so we never revisit choices — that's what turns 2ⁿ⁻¹ routes
into about n²/2 additions. (Working bottom-up instead gives the answer directly at the apex.)

## In Code

1. `max_sums` holds the best totals for the current row, updated in place.
2. For each new row `i`: first set the new right edge from the old right edge; then, from right to left,
   update each interior cell from `max(max_sums[j], max_sums[j−1])` — right to left so the values still
   needed from the row above aren't overwritten yet; then add the edge values.
3. Return the maximum of the final `max_sums`.

## Complexity

O(n²) for n rows — one step per cell. Space O(n) — a single row of totals.
