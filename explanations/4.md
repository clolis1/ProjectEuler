# Problem 4: Largest Palindrome Product

https://projecteuler.net/problem=4

**Answer:** 906609 · **Solution:** `P4.java` · **Runtime:** < 0.01 s

## Problem

A palindromic number reads the same both ways. The largest palindrome made from the product of two
2-digit numbers is 9009 = 91 × 99.

Find the largest palindrome made from the product of two 3-digit numbers.

## In Plain Terms

We're looking for the biggest number that (a) reads the same forwards and backwards and (b) is
some three-digit number times another three-digit number. There are about 800,000 pairs to try, but
we don't need most of them: start from the biggest numbers and work down, and as soon as a product is
smaller than the best palindrome found so far, nothing further down that row can beat it — so skip ahead.

## In Theory

The answer is the largest element of the intersection of two sets: palindromes, and products i × j with
100 ≤ i, j ≤ 999. Searching downward allows **pruning**:

- For a fixed i, products i × j shrink as j decreases. Once i × j ≤ best, stop the inner loop.
- The first palindrome found for a given i (counting j down) is the largest for that i, so stop there too.
- Once i × 999 ≤ best, no smaller i can win either, so stop the outer loop.

The winner is 913 × 993 = 906609.

## In Code

1. Outer loop: `i` from 999 down, while `i × 999 > largest_palindrome`.
2. Inner loop: `j` from 999 down, while `i × j > largest_palindrome`.
3. If `Digits.isPalindrome(i*j)` (compares characters from both ends of the number's string),
   record it and break to the next `i`.

## Complexity

Worst case O(n²) for n three-digit numbers, but the pruning means only a small fraction of the
~810,000 pairs are checked. Space O(1).

## Further Thoughts

Every 6-digit palindrome abccba = 100001a + 10010b + 1100c is divisible by 11, so one of the two factors
must be a multiple of 11. Stepping one loop by 11 cuts the work by another factor of 11.
