# Problem 5: Smallest Multiple

https://projecteuler.net/problem=5

**Answer:** 232792560 · **Solution:** `P5.java` · **Runtime:** < 0.01 s

## Problem

2520 is the smallest number that can be divided by each of the numbers from 1 to 10 without any remainder.

What is the smallest positive number that is evenly divisible by all of the numbers from 1 to 20?

## In Plain Terms

For a number to be divisible by 8, it needs three 2s in its makeup (8 = 2 × 2 × 2). To be divisible by 9,
it needs two 3s. So go through 1 to 20, note the most of each prime any single number needs, and multiply
just those together. Anything less would leave some number on the list unable to divide it; anything more
is wasted.

## In Theory

The answer is the **least common multiple** lcm(1, 2, …, 20). Using prime factorizations:

```
lcm = Π p^(largest exponent of p in any of 1..20)
    = 2⁴ × 3² × 5 × 7 × 11 × 13 × 17 × 19
    = 16 × 9 × 5 × 7 × 11 × 13 × 17 × 19
    = 232792560
```

16 = 2⁴ is the largest power of 2 up to 20, and 9 = 3² the largest power of 3; every other prime appears
only to the first power.

## In Code

1. For each `i` from 2 to 20, get its factorization with `Primes.getPrimeFactors(i)`.
2. Merge into `final_list`, keeping the **maximum** exponent seen for each prime.
3. Multiply out p^exponent for every entry and return the product.

(The same logic lives in `Divisors.findLCM` for reuse.)

## Complexity

O(n√n) for factoring each number up to n by trial division — trivial for n = 20. Space O(π(n)), one
entry per prime up to n.

## Further Thoughts

A shortcut without factoring anything: for each prime p ≤ n, multiply in the largest power p^k ≤ n.
