# Problem 3: Largest Prime Factor

https://projecteuler.net/problem=3

**Answer:** 6857 · **Solution:** `P3.java` · **Runtime:** < 0.01 s

## Problem

The prime factors of 13195 are 5, 7, 13 and 29.

What is the largest prime factor of the number 600851475143?

## In Plain Terms

Every whole number can be broken down into a product of primes — the "atoms" of multiplication — in
exactly one way. To find them, keep dividing the number by the smallest thing that goes into it evenly.
Each time a division works, the number shrinks, and whatever is left at the end is the biggest prime.
Because the number shrinks so quickly, we only ever try a few thousand divisors, even though the
starting number is in the hundreds of billions.

## In Theory

This is **trial division**. Divide out each factor completely, smallest first:

```
600851475143 ÷ 71   = 8462696833
8462696833   ÷ 839  = 10086647
10086647     ÷ 1471 = 6857
```

Two facts make it work:

- If we always divide out the smallest divisor first, every divisor we find is prime — any composite
  divisor would have had a smaller prime factor that we'd already removed.
- Once the trial divisor passes √(what's left), the remainder must be prime. If it had two factors,
  at least one would be ≤ its square root, and we'd have found it.

So 6857 is left over and is the largest prime factor.

## In Code

1. `Primes.getPrimeFactors(long)` builds a map of prime → multiplicity:
   - divides out all 2s, then all 3s;
   - tries `i = 5, 6, 7, …` up to the square root of the remaining value, dividing out each factor
     completely and shrinking the square-root bound as the value shrinks;
   - adds whatever is left (if not 1) as the final prime.
2. `P3` walks the map's keys and keeps the largest.

## Complexity

O(√p) where p is the second-largest prime factor — the loop stops once the trial divisor passes the
square root of what remains. Here that's about 1,500 trial divisions. Space O(number of prime factors).
