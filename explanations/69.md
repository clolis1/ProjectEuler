# Problem 69: Totient Maximum

https://projecteuler.net/problem=69

**Answer:** 510510 · **Solution:** `P69.java` · **Runtime:** < 0.01 s

## Problem

Euler's Totient function, φ(n) [sometimes called the phi function], is used to determine the number of numbers
less than n which are relatively prime to n. For example, as 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, and 8, are all less than nine and
relatively prime to nine, φ(9) = 6.

```
n    Relatively Prime    φ(n)   n/φ(n)
2    1                   1      2
3    1,2                 2      1.5
4    1,3                 2      2
5    1,2,3,4             4      1.25
6    1,5                 2      3
7    1,2,3,4,5,6         6      1.1666...
8    1,3,5,7             4      2
9    1,2,4,5,7,8         6      1.5
10   1,3,7,9             4      2.5
```

It can be seen that n = 6 produces a maximum n/φ(n) for n ≤ 10.

Find the value of n ≤ 1,000,000 for which n/φ(n) is a maximum.

## In Plain Terms

φ(n) counts how many numbers below n share no factor with n. To make that count *small* compared to n, n should
have lots of different small prime factors — each one knocks out a chunk of candidates (a factor of 2 knocks out
half, 3 a third of what's left, and so on). So the answer is simply 2 × 3 × 5 × 7 × … for as long as the product
stays under a million.

## In Theory

From the product formula for the totient:

```
n / φ(n) = Π over primes p | n of  p / (p − 1)
```

The ratio depends only on **which** primes divide n, not how often, and each factor p/(p−1) is larger for smaller
p. So the maximum below a bound is the largest **primorial** under it:

```
2 × 3 × 5 × 7 × 11 × 13 × 17 = 510510      (× 19 = 9699690 is too big)
```

## In Code

Multiply primes in increasing order (`Primes.getSetPrimesUpUntil`) while the product stays below 1,000,000.

## Complexity

O(k) for k primes in the product (7 here). Space O(π(10⁴)) for the prime set.
