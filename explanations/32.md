# Problem 32: Pandigital Products

https://projecteuler.net/problem=32

**Answer:** 45228 · **Solution:** `P32.java` · **Runtime:** ~0.02 s

## Problem

We shall say that an n-digit number is pandigital if it makes use of all the digits 1 to n exactly once; for
example, the 5-digit number, 15234, is 1 through 5 pandigital.

The product 7254 is unusual, as the identity, 39 × 186 = 7254, containing multiplicand, multiplier, and
product is 1 through 9 pandigital.

Find the sum of all products whose multiplicand/multiplier/product identity can be written as a 1 through 9
pandigital.

HINT: Some products can be obtained in more than one way so be sure to only include it once in your sum.

## In Plain Terms

We need multiplication facts like 39 × 186 = 7254 that use each of the digits 1–9 exactly once across the
whole equation. Counting digits rules out almost everything: the product has to be four digits long, which
means the two numbers being multiplied are either 1-digit × 4-digit or 2-digit × 3-digit. That's a small
enough search to try directly.

## In Theory

If the factors have a and b digits, the product has a + b − 1 or a + b digits. All nine digits used means
a + b + (product digits) = 9, which forces the product to have exactly **4 digits** and a + b = 5:
**1 × 4** or **2 × 3**.

A 9-character string contains each of 1–9 iff it contains all of them — no room for repeats or zeros.

Seven products qualify: 4396, 5346, 5796, 6952, 7254, 7632 and 7852. Some, like 5796 = 12 × 483 = 42 × 138,
appear twice, so products go into a set.

## In Code

1. `i` from 1 to 99 and `j` from 100 to 9999, keeping only products between 1000 and 9999 (the inner loop
   breaks once the product passes 9999).
2. Concatenate `i`, `j` and the product; if the string contains every digit 1–9, add the product to a `TreeSet`.
3. Sum the set.

The digit-length combinations that can't be pandigital (like 1-digit × 3-digit, which makes 8 characters)
fail the contains check on their own.

## Complexity

About 99 × 9,900 pairs before pruning, far fewer after. Space O(number of products).
